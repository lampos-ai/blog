The problem

Every model is provisioned for its peak and holds that hardware through its trough

A GPU fleet already runs many models. The constraint is how each one is allocated: a model is provisioned a slice of hardware up front and holds it continuously, whether or not requests are arriving.

Allocation has to work that way today, because loading a large model takes minutes — five to ten for a 70B-class model, and longer as you move into the hundreds of billions of parameters. That is far longer than a traffic spike lasts. So every model's provision is sized against its busiest hour and held continuously, regardless of actual demand.

Utilization ends up capped by something other than demand. Not by how busy the silicon is, but by how slowly a model can be brought up.

Activation

Seconds to activate. Nothing held while idle.

All models are available simultaneously. When a request arrives, Lampos activates the target model on-demand — from idle to serving in seconds. The following are measured results on production-class models.

ModelClassActivationMemory held while idle

Deactivation is close to instant — under a second on the models measured — so the expensive direction is the only one that matters, and it costs seconds rather than minutes.

Serving performance

Once live, it serves at full speed

Fast activation would be worthless if the model came back slower. It doesn't. Against the plain engine on the same hardware, throughput and latency both land between 0.99× and 1.01× — a band narrow enough that the differences are measurement noise, not a performance cost.

Output is checked token for token on every run, not sampled or scored for similarity. An activated model returns the identical sequence the original produced. Nothing about activation is visible to the customer sending the request.

When activation costs seconds, a model's hardware can follow its traffic instead of being provisioned against its peak. Lampos runs underneath vLLM and SGLang on H100, H200 and A100, and has been validated through 235B-parameter mixture-of-experts models and sparse-attention architectures. It sits below your serving layer, not in place of it.

The fastest way to evaluate it is to point it at the model you would least expect to move — the biggest one, the one currently holding dedicated hardware. Activation time, idle footprint and output parity are all things you can verify in an afternoon on your own fleet.
